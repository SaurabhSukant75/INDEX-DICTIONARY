02 / The conceptPlain language

What is the attention economy?

It is an environment where human attention is scarce, valuable, and competed for.

What is scarce?

Your time, focus, curiosity, and mental energy. A day has only so many waking hours.

What is valuable?

The chance to show you a message, an ad, a video, or something that influences what you do next.

Core idea: when attention creates value, products are designed to attract it—and often to keep it.

06 / Two sidesTrade-offs matter

Attention is not always the enemy.

When it helps

Learning tools, urgent alerts, meaningful communities, creative discovery, and useful recommendations can earn attention for good reasons.

When it harms

Misleading notifications, endless feeds, outrage bait, and opaque incentives can pull attention away from personal priorities.

The question isn't “How much attention?”
It's “Attention in service of what?”

Good design respects intent, makes stopping easy, and creates value beyond time spent.

Evaluate outcomes, not just metrics06
07 / System behaviorMeasure the right thing

What gets measured shapes what gets built.

If a team treats session duration as the main sign of success, it may optimize for longer sessions—even when a shorter session would better serve the person.

A proxy metric is not the goal itself. Pair engagement measures with user outcomes, satisfaction, and the ability to leave when finished.

Beware of proxy goals07
